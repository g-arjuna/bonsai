## 1. Strategic scope and standards

Wireless assurance is a user-journey and shared-medium problem, not an interface-up problem. Join AP/radio/BSS, association/roam, wired uplink/VLAN, controller/control vs data tunnel, power chain, NAC/AAA, DHCP/DNS and service experience. Standalone APs, on-prem controller-managed centrally switched or locally switched APs, embedded/virtual controller clusters, cloud-managed distributed APs, mesh/backhaul, branch/home gateways are distinct deployment profiles. Management/control reachability is not evidence of client forwarding. AP failure, controller failure, cloud visibility gap and site WAN failure must remain distinguishable.

- [IEEE 802.11 project timeline, 2026-10-27](https://www.ieee802.org/11/Reports/802.11_Timelines.htm): 802.11be-2024 (Wi-Fi 7) published July 2025; 802.11bn (UHR/Wi-Fi 8) D2.10, projected May 2028 publication, not final. 802.11-2024 integrates 802.11ax; Wi-Fi 6E is 6 GHz extension of ax, not a new PHY. Wi-Fi 4/5 n/ac remain relevant for mixed cohorts. Model negotiated rather than advertised capabilities: band, channel/width, PHY/MCS/NSS/GI, OFDMA/RU, MU-MIMO, BSS colour, TWT, 11be MLO, 320 MHz, puncturing, multiple RUs, 4096-QAM and link mode. 11k/v/r neighbor reports/steering/fast transition do not guarantee successful roam. 11bh randomized/changing MAC addresses and privacy amendments reinforce no assumption of persistent user identity. Track future 11bn features only as optional versioned evidence; no standards claim based on prototypes.
- [OpenConfig Wi-Fi models](https://github.com/openconfig/public/tree/master/release/models/wifi), [access-points schema](https://openconfig.net/projects/models/schemadocs/yangdoc/openconfig-access-points.html) (AP 1.2.0, MAC 1.5.0, PHY 1.4.1 as reviewed): AP-manager/access-points/PHY/MAC/types and BSSID discovery; architecture agnostic, model availability != implementation by actual WLC. [BBF TR-181](https://device-data-model.broadband-forum.org/) Device.WiFi.Radio/SSID/AccessPoint/AssociatedDevice and DataElements/EasyMesh for CPE/managed multi-AP, TR-369 USP or CWMP via approved existing ACS/USP management interface; no management writes. [RFC 5415](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5415.html) CAPWAP control/data, [5416](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5416.html) 802.11 binding: observe tunnel/session state from vendor, do not impersonate or terminate CAPWAP.
- [Cisco 6 GHz/Wi-Fi 7 migration](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless/catalyst-9800-series-wireless-controllers/223061-migrate-to-wi-fi-7-and-6ghz.html), [AFC FAQ](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/products/collateral/networking/wireless/access-points/automated-frequency-coordination-faq.html): country/regulatory domain, DFS/CAC/radar, 6 GHz power class (LPI/SP/VLP where permitted), AFC grant/expiry/allowed channel and power where applicable, channel changes, RRM actions and WPA3/PMF/security compatibility. Do not assume US rules apply worldwide or that absence of 6 GHz is a fault. Do not infer compliance without country/platform/regulatory evidence.
- Campus: IEEE 802.1X port-based NAC, 802.1Q VLAN/bridging/STP, 802.1AB LLDP (incl power via MDI), 802.3af/at/bt PoE, 802.3bz multi-gigabit, 802.3az EEE; vendor CDP, stack/VSF/MLAG and fabric access (VXLAN/EVPN, SDA etc) optional profiles. [RFC 2865](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2865.html) RADIUS, [2866](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2866.html) accounting, [3579](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3579.html) EAP over RADIUS, [3748](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3748.html) EAP, [5176](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5176.html) CoA/Disconnect, [6614](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6614.html) RADIUS/TLS, [9765](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9765.html) RADIUS/1.1 Experimental (TLS ALPN, not all deployments). Observe existing authentication/accounting and CoA outcomes; never become AAA server or issue CoA. [RFC 3621](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3621.html) legacy POWER-ETHERNET-MIB (class enum only old classes, not full bt), [RFC 3414](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3414.html) SNMPv3 USM; prefer authenticated read paths, no unauthenticated traps as authoritative.

Metric math: calculate rates from valid deltas and actual elapsed source times; airtime busy fraction from compatible busy/active time within the same channel and sample epoch (not sum of different radios). Linux cfg80211 tx_retries/tx_failed count MPDUs, tx_packets counts MSDUs/MMPDUs, so retry/tx_packets is not a universal frame retry percentage. Preserve vendor definitions, use attempted/retried same-layer denominator only if known; otherwise retry attempts/s and failures/s, no clamp or fake percent. RSSI dBm is logarithmic: report quantiles/minimum and measured perspective, linear-power averaging only if meaningful; SNR=signal-noise when same sample/band/units. PHY negotiated rate != payload throughput; high busy with low bytes may reflect contention/interference, small frames or low-rate airtime. Do not identify interference type or location from retries alone. TWT and sleep can reduce observed activity without poor service. Do not add per-link and aggregate MLD bytes. Roam time requires actual endpoints/stage timestamps, no inferred millisecond duration from 3-minute snapshots. Distinguish no clients from no data.

## 4. PoE, energy and access security (core differentiators)

PoE PSE max vs PD power available at cable end (IEEE class/type, channel loss and negotiation): af Type1 15.4/12.95 W, at Type2 30/25.5 W, bt Type3 up to 60/51 W, Type4 up to 90/71.3 W. Actual power class, cable condition, injector, switch supply/stack and redundancy determine effective budget. Avoid equating class maximum with consumption; proprietary power modes differ. [Cisco 9178 data sheet](https://www.cisco.com/site/us/en/products/collateral/networking/wireless/access-points/wireless-9178-series-access-points-ds.html) documents release-specific 802.3bt class 6 full radio mode max 47 W vs 802.3at 25.5 W with reduced spatial streams/uplink/USB, and af staging radios off; [AP power requirements](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless/catalyst-9100-access-points/215467-cisco-access-point-power-requirements-qu.html) warns support depends on exact model/software/standard. This is a real diagnostic path: new Wi-Fi 7 AP degraded after switch/PSU change, not necessarily RF interference. Model effective power mode and thermal throttling separately. Validate requested, allocated and measured PSE port W, PD input W and source (not interchangeable), per-pair faults, negotiation LLDP/CDP, port priority, dual-feed/injector and PSU N-1. For a switch with 740 W effective PoE budget, 600 W allocated, 390 W measured, allocation headroom=140 W vs measured headroom=350 W; not all 350 W necessarily allocatable. Track load-shed/denials and impacted AP/phones/cameras. Energy kWh = integral measured W over valid hours /1000, disclose gaps and meter boundary; do not double count switch input plus its PD load or use class watts as actual usage. Report site/closet/AP energy, idle/peak, energy per delivered GB or client-hour only with matched coverage; optional tariff/emissions factors user-provided and explicitly scoped. TWT/client battery not inferable from AP power. Read [OpenConfig interfaces/PoE/dot1x](https://openconfig.net/projects/models/schemadocs/yangdoc/openconfig-interfaces.html) /interfaces/interface/ethernet/poe/state/power-used,power-class, vendor native richer power budget/port counters, SNMPv3 RFC3621 group+port indexes mapped independently to ifIndex (not equal by assumption), LLDP power TLVs. Reuse BN-025-08 environmental shared domain, BN-026-04 directional rates; no power-saving actions or PoE reset.

NAC path: supplicant -> AP/switch authenticator -> AAA/NAC -> authorization applied (VLAN/role/ACL) -> DHCP/IP/DNS -> application. 802.11 association, 802.1X/EAP, 4-way handshake, RADIUS response, policy installed and real forwarding are distinct stages, ordering depends on security mode. For wired multi-auth/voice+data, physical port up and one authorized client do not imply all clients authorized. Collect server pool metrics and NAS-side outcome (accept does not prove enforcement), accounting and access session state, timeouts/retransmit, rejection and certificate issues. [Cisco ISE pxGrid](https://developer.cisco.com/docs/pxgrid/overview/) session directory, RADIUS failures, health via REST/WSS, scoped read-only subscriptions; MnT REST requires elevated monitoring groups, evaluate least privilege before use. [ClearPass syslog](https://arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/ClearPass/6.10/PolicyManager/Content/CPPM_UserGuide/Admin/syslogTargets.html) session/audit/event export via approved secure relay (native TLS not assumed); supported read API, FreeRADIUS existing accounting/log exporter and Mist Access Assurance later fixtures. Do not ingest passwords, EAP blobs, RADIUS shared secrets, certificates/private keys, identity store dumps, raw packet contents. No authentication relay, enforcement, CoA, deauth, port shutdown, key export or forced reauth. Observe policy changes as evidence only.

## 6. Distributed collector/core design

One product with acquisition adapters (source manifest/version, credentials/scope, endpoint/region, read-only operations, supported metric/cadence and license), canonical observation ABI, edge normalization and core projection. Local collectors near WLCs, AP sites, NAC/switches; cloud API connector per org/region, elected owner/lease and shared vendor quotas, not per-site duplicate requests. Webhook ingress may be approved regional gateway relaying authenticated events; no requirement to expose every collector publicly. AP inventory may be learned from authorized controller without IP/credentials; explicit child entity approval/scope. Source authentication via existing collector principal and trusted binding, with constrained child mapping; subject identity never hostname/MAC/IP alone. Epoch and fencing for controller HA, AP reboot, source failover/lease handoff, snapshot generation, duplicate push+poll, old owner late writes. When controller unavailable, retain last facts as stale; do not synthesize every AP/client disconnect.

Pipeline:
1. Ingress adapters gNMI/MDT (separate framing), authorized REST polling, WSS/MQTT/GPB, signed webhook, local exporter or SNMPv3; bounds before decode, TLS/token/cert/rotation, schema/size/cardinality, per-org quotas, safe egress, webhook replay protection and topic authorization. Acknowledge only at explicit durable boundary when supported; vendor delivery ack != core projection. Stream gap -> observable and bounded reconcile snapshots, not pretend lossless.
2. Edge canonicalize nested AP/radio/BSS/session facts, typed per-field timestamps and identity; reduce/filter by site/SSID/metric policy BEFORE durable archive (privacy redaction, HMAC pseudonym scoped/rotated, no identities in metric labels or log). Source snapshot pages with completeness, record count and watermark; incomplete scan not authoritative deletion. Preserve event identity, roam and NAC/PoE transitions and evidence gaps in reserved critical lane. Dedup only semantically identical events; no generic debounce of distinct state transitions.
3. Edge bounded windows: radio/AP/site/SSID aggregates, event-time watermark with late arrivals, compatible numerator/denominator, duration-weighted gauges and histograms/sketches with counts, min/max/p95 etc. Keep top offenders (bounded with coverage), short incident windows on policy and source support; per-client summaries selectively, bounded session cache with TTL and explicit eviction/gap. No unbounded per-device or per-client metric label/cardinality. Full snapshots and deltas have different loss/merge semantics. Durable segment spool, hard bytes/age/records, priority partitions, restart/replay, offline gap markers; reuse existing BN-009/019/023 work. Budget CPU/RSS/disk and source read cost at edge, core and query.
4. Core typed evidence and idempotent projection using stable observation ID/checkpoint, authoritative source scope and valid intervals. Retire stale sessions only after valid end/complete reconcile, not bus silence. Latest graph topology, attachments and summaries in Ladybug, detailed series/incident windows in bounded typed history or authorized external TSDB; one canonical graph writer, no full graph copy in every collector. Scoped query and raw dereference redacted, auth and retention apply to both. Distinguish received, journaled, projected, archived, dropped, filtered, sampled, expired. Backpressure lowers bulk sample fidelity with explicit coverage; routing/critical events have priority.
5. ## 8. Deterministic and ML use cases

- Co-channel contention vs weak RF vs hidden-node/interference: compare band/channel/neighbor overlap, busy fraction, per-link RSSI/SNR, retries, load, MCS and goodput; distinguish weak signal from high utilization and unsupported spectrum data. No RF localization without calibrated measurements.
- Sticky client/roam failures: time-scoped attachment and neighbor candidates, steering/FT outcomes and time, client capability cohort; do not infer missed roam solely from RSSI. MLO link loss vs session loss.
- AP unavailable vs controller/cloud blind vs upstream WAN, mesh backhaul congestion vs client RF, CAPWAP tunnel failure vs local switching still works.
- PoE denial/PSU failure/thermal throttling, mGig downgrade or bad cable vs RF congestion; power budget N-1 and energy forecast; no auto-power down.
- 802.1X/MAB/DHCP/DNS failures, identity provider latency, mismatched VLAN or STP blocked access, intermittent policy changes and repeated auth loops; separate cause/correlation, scoped session evidence.
- Capacity: time-of-day/weekday/site/SSID/band baselines, airtime fairness and overloaded APs vs throughput, client-count growth, busy-hour energy and headroom, service-level failure forecasts. Robust quantiles/change point before ML/GNN; feature masks for unsupported, sample coverage and source differences. Edge/session/radio time-series with independent labels (tickets, actual observed outcomes, maintenance). Train/test grouped by site/client cohort/time/failure and vendor, no post-event leakage or identifiers, compare deterministic/seasonal baselines; precision/recall, false alerts/AP-day, lead time, calibration, RCA top-k/abstention, false attribution and resource cost. Synthetic replay tests plumbing, not real prediction.
